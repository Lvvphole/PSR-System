## SECTION 1: TERRITORY MARKET CONDITIONS
## February 2026
### Overall Market Characterization
The Northeast Atlanta construction market is in
a period of geographic redistribution rather than
broad decline. Metro Atlanta recorded 19,529 new
residential construction permits in 2025, a 12%
year-over-year decrease. However that headline
number obscures significant variation across
the counties in this territory.
The key insight for contractor conversations:
the overall market contracted but the territory
you cover contains both the highest volume county
in the metro and the fastest growing county
in the metro. Positioning matters more than
ever — contractors who are active in the right
submarkets are busy regardless of the headline.
### County-by-County Breakdown
GWINNETT COUNTY
2025 permits: 2,962 — highest volume in Metro Atlanta.
Year-over-year change: -29%.
What this means for your portfolio: Overall volume
contracted sharply, suggesting saturation in some
corridors. However Gwinnett posted 1,028
higher-value permits (homes above $500,000) in 2025,
doubling from 513 in 2024. Premium residential
construction is accelerating even as overall volume
shrinks. Contractors in Gwinnett working on
higher-end builds are likely busier and spending
more per project than lower-end volume builders.
Insight angle for Gwinnett contractors: The volume
play is cooling but the premium play is hot.
Contractors moving upmarket in Gwinnett are
seeing larger per-project material budgets.
If they're still buying at volume pricing levels
they may be leaving margin on the table.
FORSYTH COUNTY
2025 permits: 1,277 — down 11% year-over-year.
Median home price: approximately $600,000.
What this means for your portfolio: Forsyth
remains a premium market with strong fundamentals.
The Northward Shift continues — buyers priced
out of Alpharetta and Milton are moving north
into Forsyth, sustaining demand for new
construction at higher price points. South Forsyth
is the most active submarket, bordering
Johns Creek and Alpharetta. North Forsyth
is developing rapidly with more land available
and newer construction in the $450,000 to
$600,000 range. The Floating Homestead
Exemption (HB 717) took effect January 2026,
capping assessment increases at 4% annually —
this protects long-term affordability and
sustains buyer demand for new construction.
Insight angle for Forsyth contractors:
Buyers here are paying premium prices and
are quality-sensitive. Material selection
reflects on the builder's reputation.
This is a market where product quality and
finish level matter more than lowest cost.
HALL COUNTY (Gainesville, Flowery Branch,
Buford northern corridor)
2025 permits: 1,547 — up 14% year-over-year.
Multi-year trend: growth continuing from 2024.
What this means for your portfolio: Hall County
is the strongest growth story in this territory
and one of the few high-volume growth counties
in the entire Metro. Gainesville and Flowery Branch
are seeing expanding developer interest.
This is structural demand driven by Lake Lanier
access, affordability relative to South Forsyth,
and improving infrastructure. Contractors
active in Hall County are likely seeing
increasing project volume heading into 2026.
Insight angle for Hall County contractors:
This is the growth market in the territory.
Contractors here are scaling up.
The question to ask is whether their
material supply relationships are scaling
with them or whether they're still operating
with suppliers sized for smaller volume.
BARROW COUNTY (Buford corridor)
2025 permits: 1,060 — up 20% year-over-year.
Three-year trend: +24% in 2023, +12% in 2024,
+20% in 2025.
What this means for your portfolio: Barrow is
the most consistently expanding market in the
region by percentage growth. Three consecutive
years of strong growth suggests structural
demand rather than a blip. Contractors expanding
into Barrow from Gwinnett are worth identifying
and cultivating now before they establish
supplier relationships elsewhere.
Insight angle for Barrow-active contractors:
Three-year sustained growth is a rare signal
in this market. Contractors positioned here
early are winning volume. The infrastructure
and school district investment following
population growth creates a multi-year
building cycle.
JOHNS CREEK / ROSWELL / SUWANEE
These markets sit at the intersection of
Fulton, Forsyth, and Gwinnett county dynamics.
Roswell and Johns Creek are infill and
remodel-heavy markets as land for new
construction is constrained. Suwanee
benefits from Gwinnett spillover and
maintains strong demand for both new
construction and renovation.
Insight angle: Remodelers active in
Johns Creek and Roswell are working in
a market where homeowners are equity-rich
and renovation budgets are larger than
average. Material quality and finish level
expectations are high.

## SECTION 2: MATERIAL PRICING INTELLIGENCE
## February 2026
### The Tariff Story — Most Important Context
### for All Contractor Conversations
This is the single most important market
development affecting every contractor in
your portfolio right now. Understanding it
better than they do is your primary
Challenger insight for February.
Current tariff situation as of February 2026:
- Steel and aluminum: 50% tariff in effect
  since June 2025. Domestic producers have
  used tariff cover to raise their own prices.
  Steel up 22.8% year-over-year as of
  August 2025. Aluminum up roughly 40%
  following tariff increases.
- Softwood lumber: 10% tariff currently.
  Canadian lumber (85% of US softwood supply)
  faces 14.5% existing anti-dumping duties
  with potential increase to 34.5% under review.
  Despite tariff risk, current softwood lumber
  prices are actually down approximately 8%
  year-over-year due to weak construction demand —
  but this could reverse quickly if tariff
  increases are enacted or demand recovers.
- Copper: 50% tariff took effect August 2025.
- Gypsum/drywall: 71% of US gypsum supply
  comes from Mexico. Mexican tariffs create
  drywall price risk.
- Overall residential construction input
  costs: up 4.2% year-over-year as of late 2025.
  Construction input costs are still 40%+ above
  pre-pandemic February 2020 levels.
- NAHB estimates recent tariff actions add
  approximately $10,900 per home in construction
  costs on average.
- 43% of general contractors reported at least
  one project canceled, postponed or scaled back
  in the past six months due to higher material
  costs per AGC-NCCER survey.
### Category-by-Category Pricing Conditions
LUMBER AND FRAMING
Current price direction: Down year-over-year
  approximately 8% for softwood lumber.
Atlanta market range: $3 to $6 per board foot
  for standard framing lumber.
Risk flag: Canadian lumber tariff increase
  from 14.5% to 34.5% under active review.
  If enacted, framing costs increase materially.
PSR opportunity: Lumber prices are currently
  favorable. Contractors locking in framing
  material for upcoming projects now are
  buying at the best prices available before
  potential tariff increases hit.
Insight angle: "Lumber is at a relative low
  right now with tariff increases pending.
  If you have projects starting in the next
  60-90 days, buying framing material now
  versus waiting could be meaningful savings."
CONCRETE AND MASONRY
Current price direction: Ready-mix concrete
  down 0.7% year-over-year. Bagged concrete
  prices stable to modest increases.
Risk flag: EPA kiln regulations constraining
  supply. Concrete prices projected up 4-6%
  in 2026 from regulatory pressure.
PSR opportunity: Stable pricing now, rising
  pricing ahead. Concrete and masonry for
  near-term projects should be quoted and
  committed promptly.
Atlanta benchmark: Foundation work typically
  $5.60 to $7.50 per square foot in Georgia.
ROOFING MATERIALS
Current price direction: Asphalt volatile —
  same trend expected to continue through 2026
  due to labor market tightness, shifting
  supply chains, and severe weather demand
  spikes in the Southeast.
Atlanta benchmark: Composition shingles
  $90 to $150 per square (100 sq ft).
  Metal roofing $300 to $900 per square.
Risk flag: Severe weather in Southeast
  creates periodic demand spikes that
  strain regional availability.
PSR opportunity: Roofers doing volume work
  in Northeast Atlanta benefit from having
  a reliable supply source who can commit
  to delivery timing — availability certainty
  is worth as much as price in this category.
STEEL AND METAL PRODUCTS
Current price direction: Sharply up.
Steel up 22.8% year-over-year.
Metal molding and trim up 48.2% year-over-year.
Aluminum up approximately 40%.
Copper up significantly following
  50% tariff August 2025.
Impact on contractors: GCs and builders
  using structural steel, metal framing,
  copper electrical and plumbing are
  facing significant cost pressure.
  These increases directly erode bid margins
  on fixed-price contracts.
PSR opportunity: Contractors who bid projects
  6-12 months ago using pre-tariff metal
  pricing are now absorbing cost overruns.
  This is a margin conversation worth having.
DRYWALL AND GYPSUM
Current price direction: Stable but at
  elevated levels. Tariff risk on Mexican
  gypsum (71% of US supply) remains.
PSR opportunity: Remodelers and builders
  doing interior work should be aware of
  drywall supply risk. Early commitment
  on drywall quantities for active projects
  is prudent.
APPLIANCES
Current price direction: Up due to tariffs
  on imported goods from China and other
  tariff-affected countries.
PSR opportunity: Builders doing multiple
  units should consolidate appliance
  purchases through Home Depot Pro pricing
  rather than retail. Volume discount on
  appliances across a 5-10 unit project
  is meaningful.
FLOORING — LVP
Current price direction: LVP remains
  preferred over hardwood for cost-conscious
  builds. Pricing relatively stable.
PSR opportunity: Builders and remodelers
  defaulting to LVP should be quoting
  flooring through you rather than
  flooring specialty stores. This is
  a high-capture opportunity.
INSULATION
Current price direction: Fiberglass
  still most budget-friendly. Cellulose
  experiencing higher demand-driven costs.
  Foam board faced rising costs in 2025.
PSR opportunity: Insulation is a
  natural add-on to every framing or
  roofing order for builders.
  Frequently not mentioned until
  rough-in stage — ask earlier.

## SECTION 3: LABOR MARKET CONDITIONS
Labor shortage is as significant as material
pricing for your contractors in February 2026.
Key data points:
- Construction industry needs approximately
  499,000 additional workers to meet 2026 demand
  per Associated Builders and Contractors.
- 20% of subcontractors lost construction workers
  in 2025 due to retiring workforce and
  fewer skilled younger workers.
- Construction employment expected to grow
  only 0.3% in 2026.
- Immigration enforcement concerns are
  affecting labor availability for contractors
  who rely on immigrant workforce.
- Labor costs are now rising faster than
  materials in several trade categories.
What this means for contractor conversations:
Labor shortage means projects are taking longer
and costing more to staff. Contractors who can
get materials right the first time —
no short orders, no wrong products,
no delivery delays — are protecting their
crew time and schedule. This is your
value proposition in a labor-constrained market.
Every material problem you prevent saves them
crew hours they cannot afford to waste.

## SECTION 5: SEASONAL CONTEXT
## February 2026
February in Northeast Atlanta is late
pre-season for exterior construction.
Roofing, framing, and foundation work
are beginning to pick up as weather
improves. Contractors are finalizing
project pipelines for spring starts.
Key seasonal implications:
- Roofers are quoting spring storm season
  preparation and reroof projects.
- Builders are finalizing framing material
  commitments for Q2 starts.
- Remodelers are taking on kitchen and
  bathroom projects scheduled before
  summer exterior work begins.
- Landscape and deck projects are
  being planned and quoted now.
This is the highest-value month for
upstream project entry. Contractors
finalizing spring project material lists
right now. Getting into those conversations
today means setting the material spec
before competitors have a chance to quote.
