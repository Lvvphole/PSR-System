## QUESTION 1: FULL PROJECT MATERIALS LIST
## What does a complete roofing project require?
RESIDENTIAL REROOF — TYPICAL MATERIALS LIST
- Asphalt shingles (3-tab or architectural)
- Synthetic or felt underlayment
- Ice and water shield (eaves, valleys,
  penetrations)
- Roof deck — OSB or plywood if decking
  replacement needed
- Roofing nails and staples
- Ridge cap shingles
- Starter strip shingles
- Drip edge — eave and rake
- Pipe boots and roof penetration flashings
- Step flashing and counter flashing
  at walls and chimneys
- Roof deck screws
- Ventilation — ridge vents, box vents,
  or soffit vents
- Roofing adhesive and sealant
- Dumpster or debris removal supplies
COMMERCIAL OR FLAT ROOF ADDITIONS
- TPO or modified bitumen membrane
- Insulation board
- Fasteners and plates
- Seam tape and primers
STORM DAMAGE OR INSURANCE WORK
- Decking repair lumber — 2x6 or OSB sheets
- Additional underlayment
- Flashing replacements
- Fascia board and soffit materials

## QUESTION 2: WHAT DO THEY BUY ELSEWHERE
## THAT THEY SHOULD BE BUYING FROM YOU?
HIGH PROBABILITY GAPS — categories roofers
commonly source from roofing distributors or
competitors instead of Home Depot:
Shingles in volume — roofers often have
distributor accounts with GAF, CertainTeed,
or Owens Corning direct.
Opportunity: Home Depot carries all three brands.
Pro pricing and delivery to jobsite removes
the distributor middle step for smaller orders
or emergency replenishment.
Ice and water shield — frequently purchased
at roofing supply houses.
Opportunity: Consistent availability,
same-day pickup at any district store.
Decking and lumber for repair work — roofers
doing storm or insurance work need OSB and
framing lumber but often don't think of
Home Depot first for this category.
Opportunity: This is a natural add-on to
every shingle order for storm work.
Ventilation products — ridge vents and
box vents often purchased separately at
HVAC or roofing suppliers.
Opportunity: Bundle ventilation into the
roofing materials quote proactively.
Fascia and soffit materials — frequently
sourced at lumber yards.
Opportunity: Ask about fascia and soffit
condition on every reroof conversation.
Tools and equipment — nail guns, blades,
safety equipment.
Opportunity: Home Depot Pro pricing on
tools and consumables.

## QUESTION 3: OUTCOMES THEY CARE ABOUT
MARGIN
Shingle and material cost is their largest
variable expense. A 5% reduction in material
cost on a $15,000 job is $750 in margin
recovered. Pro pricing and volume discounts
are the primary value lever.
Insight angle: Show them the per-square cost
comparison on shingles across brands.
Help them protect margin without sacrificing
product quality.
TIMELINE AND MATERIAL AVAILABILITY
Roofers work on tight weather windows.
A delayed shingle delivery on a day with
a clear forecast is a lost day of revenue.
Availability and delivery reliability matter
more than almost anything else.
Insight angle: Lead with same-day availability
and delivery to jobsite capability.
This is often more valuable than price.
NO SURPRISES ON MATERIAL QUANTITIES
Underordering on a roof mid-job creates
schedule problems and dye lot matching issues
on shingles. Roofers want accurate material
estimates the first time.
Insight angle: Offer to run material
calculations based on square footage.
Remove the math burden and reduce
the risk of short orders.
CREDIT TERMS
Roofers often get paid on completion or
on insurance check release. They are
frequently cash-flow constrained while
materials are needed upfront.
Insight angle: Home Depot commercial credit
and net terms can bridge the cash flow gap
between material purchase and payment receipt.
