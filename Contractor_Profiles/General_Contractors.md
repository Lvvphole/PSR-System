## QUESTION 1: FULL PROJECT MATERIALS LIST
## What does a GC typically need across projects?
General contractors manage scope across multiple
trade categories simultaneously. Their materials
list varies more than any other contractor type
because it reflects whatever their current
project mix requires.
COMMON RECURRING CATEGORIES FOR GCs
CONCRETE AND MASONRY
- Bagged concrete and mortar
- Concrete block and brick
- Rebar, wire mesh, and form work
- Masonry anchors and fasteners
FRAMING AND STRUCTURAL
- Dimensional lumber
- Engineered lumber and beams
- OSB and plywood sheathing
- Structural connectors and hangers
- Fasteners — nails, screws, bolts
EXTERIOR ENVELOPE
- House wrap and weather barrier
- Windows and exterior doors
- Siding materials
- Roofing materials
- Flashing and sealants
ROUGH-IN MATERIALS
- Electrical — wire, boxes, panels, conduit
- Plumbing — pipe, fittings, fixtures
- HVAC support materials
- Insulation
INTERIOR FINISH
- Drywall and joint compound
- Paint — interior and exterior
- Flooring — multiple categories
- Interior doors, trim, and hardware
- Cabinets and countertops
- Fixtures
SITE AND SAFETY
- Temporary fencing and barriers
- Safety equipment and PPE
- Tools and equipment
- Concrete cutting and grinding supplies

## QUESTION 2: WHAT DO THEY BUY ELSEWHERE
## THAT THEY SHOULD BE BUYING FROM YOU?
HIGH PROBABILITY GAPS — categories GCs
commonly source from multiple suppliers
instead of consolidating:
Electrical materials — frequently purchased
at electrical distributors like Graybar
or Rexel.
Opportunity: For standard residential and
light commercial electrical work Home Depot
Pro pricing covers the majority of what
a GC needs. Consolidation saves trip time
and account management overhead.
Plumbing materials — frequently purchased
at plumbing supply houses like Ferguson.
Opportunity: Same argument as electrical.
Standard plumbing rough-in and fixture
installation materials are available at
competitive Pro pricing with delivery.
Concrete and masonry — often purchased
at masonry supply specialists.
Opportunity: For projects requiring
bagged material in moderate volume
Home Depot delivery to jobsite is
often faster and simpler than a
masonry supplier delivery minimum.
Safety and PPE — often purchased at
safety supply distributors.
Opportunity: Home Depot carries a full
safety and PPE line at Pro pricing.
This is a recurring consumable category
that consolidates well.
Paint — frequently purchased at
Sherwin-Williams job accounts.
Opportunity: For GCs not doing high-volume
custom color work Home Depot Pro paint
pricing with delivery is competitive.
Tools and equipment — often purchased
at multiple retailers.
Opportunity: Home Depot Pro tool pricing
and tool rental program. Volume tool
purchases for a GC's crew are
a meaningful annual spend category.

## QUESTION 3: OUTCOMES THEY CARE ABOUT
ONE POINT OF CONTACT ACROSS CATEGORIES
GCs manage complexity across multiple trades
and multiple suppliers simultaneously.
The highest value a PSR can deliver to a GC
is reducing that supplier count.
Insight angle: Position yourself as the
coordinator who can cover framing, roofing,
finish, electrical rough-in, plumbing
rough-in, and paint in a single call.
The time saved on supplier management
is worth a price premium.
MARGIN PROTECTION ON BID WORK
GCs bid fixed-price contracts. Material cost
overruns and change orders erode margin
on jobs that were profitable at bid time.
Insight angle: Help them build complete
material lists at bid time. An incomplete
bid is a margin leak that starts before
the first nail is driven.
DELIVERY RELIABILITY ACROSS MULTIPLE JOBSITES
GCs often have multiple active jobsites
simultaneously. A delivery failure at one
site affects their entire schedule.
Insight angle: Nationwide delivery capability
is a genuine differentiator. Any Home Depot
in the country can fulfill an order.
For a GC with sites across the metro
this matters.
CREDIT TERMS AND PAYMENT FLEXIBILITY
GCs bill on completion milestones or
application for payment schedules.
Materials are needed before payment
is received.
Insight angle: Commercial credit with
net terms aligned to their payment
schedule removes cash flow pressure
on active projects.
SUBCONTRACTOR COORDINATION SUPPORT
GCs are often caught between a sub who
says materials weren't delivered and
a supplier who says they were.
Insight angle: Order tracking, delivery
confirmation, and a single point of
contact who answers the phone removes
a category of friction that consumes
GC management time.
