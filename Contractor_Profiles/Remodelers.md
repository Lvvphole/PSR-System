## QUESTION 2: WHAT DO THEY BUY ELSEWHERE
## THAT THEY SHOULD BE BUYING FROM YOU?
HIGH PROBABILITY GAPS — categories remodelers
commonly source from specialty suppliers
instead of Home Depot:
Tile and setting materials — often purchased
at tile specialty stores.
Opportunity: Home Depot Pro pricing on tile,
thinset, grout, and backer board as a
complete package. One stop vs multiple stops.
Plumbing fixtures and rough-in materials —
often sourced at plumbing supply houses.
Opportunity: Remodelers doing their own
plumbing rough-in can consolidate at
Home Depot. Supply lines, shutoffs,
p-traps, and drain assemblies.
Electrical materials — frequently purchased
at electrical distributors.
Opportunity: Outlets, switches, wire,
boxes, and panels for basic remodel
electrical work.
Paint — often purchased at Sherwin-Williams
or Benjamin Moore.
Opportunity: Home Depot carries premium
paint lines. Pro pricing and delivery make
the case for consolidation.
Doors and windows — often purchased at
specialty window and door dealers.
Opportunity: For standard sizes Home Depot
inventory and delivery to jobsite is
competitive on both price and availability.
Flooring — often purchased at flooring
specialty stores.
Opportunity: LVP, tile, and hardwood with
Pro pricing and delivery.

## QUESTION 3: OUTCOMES THEY CARE ABOUT
MARGIN
Remodelers bid fixed-price jobs. Material cost
overruns come directly out of their margin.
Accurate quotes and stable pricing matter
more than almost anything.
Insight angle: Help them build complete
material lists at bid time rather than
discovering missing items mid-job.
Every forgotten item costs them margin.
NO SURPRISES — MATERIAL CONSISTENCY
Remodelers working on occupied homes cannot
afford mid-project material delays or
substitutions. A tile that goes out of stock
mid-bathroom is a client relationship problem.
Insight angle: Check stock levels before
they commit to a material on a bid.
Proactively flag anything with availability risk.
TIMELINE
Remodelers often manage multiple jobs
simultaneously. A material delay on one job
cascades across their entire schedule.
Insight angle: Delivery to jobsite on a
confirmed date is worth a price premium
to a remodeler managing multiple active jobs.
CLIENT PRESENTATION
Remodelers in the Northeast Atlanta market
often work with homeowners who care about
brand and finish quality. Material selections
reflect on the remodeler's reputation.
Insight angle: Know which product lines
photograph well and hold up to homeowner
scrutiny. This is relationship currency
with remodelers who care about referrals.
CREDIT TERMS
Remodelers often collect a deposit and
draw schedule from homeowners. Materials
need to be purchased before draw payments
arrive.
Insight angle: Commercial credit bridges
the gap between material purchase
and draw receipt.
